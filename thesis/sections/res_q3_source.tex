\section{Does the Source of the Demonstrations Matter?}\label{sec:res:source}

Section~\ref{sec:res:internalise} distilled the policy from its own shielded rollouts. An obvious
alternative is to distil from a stronger, purpose-built expert. This section reports that
comparison, and it is the sharpest result in this chapter.

A sampling-based motion planner was implemented as a privileged expert: RRT-Connect in joint space
with clearance inflation, followed by inverse kinematics and playback as operational-space deltas.
Unlike the shielded policy, this expert is self-safe by construction rather than by correction. It
produced 318 demonstrations that succeeded and displaced nothing, across 18 of the 24 scenes~---
six scenes it could not solve at all.

The planner-distilled policy is statistically indistinguishable from the undistilled base policy on
both metrics. It learned essentially nothing.

This is a controlled contrast. Both students start from the same checkpoint, are trained with the
same objective, learning rate and batch size, are matched on gradient steps to within one percent,
and are evaluated by the same code on the same 24 scenes and the same held-out initial states. The
only difference is where the demonstrations came from. Distilling safety from a foreign expert
failed; distilling it from the policy's own shielded rollouts succeeded.

\paragraph{Interactive distillation, and the diagnostic that located its failure.}
Before the offline comparison reported above, the classical expert was distilled interactively in
the DAgger manner: the student drives, the expert labels the states the student visits, and the
aggregated dataset is retrained. This is the textbook remedy for exactly the coverage problem
identified below, and it made matters worse. Collision rose monotonically across rounds, from 44\%
to 75\%, with success pinned near zero.

The cause was not the coverage principle but a violated precondition. DAgger's correctness argument
requires a Markov oracle $\pi^{*}(s)$: the expert's label must be a function of the state it is
shown. The classical expert was implemented as a latched phase machine carrying hidden internal
state, so when the student drove erratically the expert's phase desynchronised from the observation
and the same image received contradictory labels across rounds. Behaviour cloning averaged them.

Isolating this required ruling out the alternative, that the imitation machinery itself was broken.
Hard-overfitting behaviour cloning on a single clean demonstration and evaluating on that same
episode returned success $1.0$ with zero collisions, establishing that normalisation, the
observation pipeline and adapter capacity were all sound, and that the fault lay in the labels. The
expert was then rewritten as a stateless reactive oracle, inferring its phase from observables at
every call~--- grasp from the measured gripper finger separation, transport from end-effector
height~--- and validated against the original on the same episodes, where it produced identical
outcomes episode-for-episode.

The rewrite made the expert Markov, and distillation still failed. That is what moved the
investigation from the interactive protocol to the teacher itself, and ultimately to the matched
offline comparison reported above. It is recorded here because the negative is easy to misattribute:
an interactive method failing against a non-Markov expert is a statement about the expert, not about
DAgger or about coverage.

\paragraph{The expert is not self-safe.}
One measurement sharpens what the planner's demonstrations actually contain. Running the classical
expert as the driver on spatial task~0 with the shield active gives $5/8$ success and $0/8$
collisions. Running the same expert unshielded gives $7/8$ success and $5/8$ collisions, with
contact from arm links, gripper, held object and scene objects alike. The safety of its
demonstrations is therefore a property of the shield correcting it, not of the expert itself, and
the label ``safe by construction'' applies to the planned path rather than to the executed
trajectory.

It is tempting to conclude from this that the corrections depend on state the student cannot
observe, and that behaviour cloning must therefore average contradictory targets. That explanation
was tested directly and rejected (Section~\ref{sec:res:mechanism}): the planner's demonstrations
are roughly twice as well determined by the observation as the shielded ones at every matching
radius. The teacher that fails to distil has the \emph{cleaner} observation-to-action mapping. Some
other account is required.

\paragraph{The mechanism is state coverage.}
The account that survives concerns where the supervision sits rather than who produced it, and it
is measurable. Figure~\ref{fig:state_coverage} takes the base policy's own visited states and asks
how closely each demonstration set approaches them.

The comparison must control for sample density, and this is not a detail. Planner episodes run to
horizon 900 against 300 for a shielded rollout, so per scene the planner contributes roughly thirty
times more state points, and nearest-neighbour coverage rises monotonically with density. Compared
without that control the two sets are nearly indistinguishable, at 37.7\% against 32.8\%.
Subsampling both to equal size per scene, averaged over 30 resamples, separates them: the shielded
rollouts cover 37.7\% of the policy's own states within 2\,cm against the planner's 22.8\%, and
cover better in 15 of the 18 scenes holding both demonstration types.

Panel~(b) of Figure~\ref{fig:state_coverage} shows the shape of the difference. The planner is a
scripted controller and traces near-identical paths from a given initial condition, so its
demonstrations form tight deterministic bands; the states the policy actually visits are diffuse
and lie largely between and around them. The two distributions are not disjoint~--- the claim is
not that the planner visits a different region of the workspace~--- but the planner's density is
concentrated where the policy is not.

This is the distinction the contrast isolates, and it is narrower than ``foreign experts cannot
teach''. A foreign expert can teach safety: SAFE-GIL clones an MPC or PID controller and obtains
substantially safer policies, having first used reachability analysis to force that fixed expert to
demonstrate recovery from the states a learner's errors would produce. The planner tested here
received no such treatment. Its trajectories were generated from its own initial conditions and
were never conditioned on where $\pi_{0.5}$ fails. That, rather than its foreignness, is what
separates the two teachers.

Two caveats bound the measurement. Coverage is computed on end-effector position, three of the
eight observed dimensions, so it does not speak to joint configuration. And it is a property of the
demonstration sets rather than a demonstration that coverage is what the policy learned from~---
the causal claim rests on the controlled contrast in Table~\ref{tab:demo_source_comparison}, with
the coverage measurement as its most direct available evidence.

\paragraph{Coverage limitations of the teacher itself.}
Six of the 24 scenes contained no planner demonstrations, so roughly 30 of the 120 evaluation
rollouts test the expert's coverage rather than the distillation itself. That gap is not random:
per-task inspection on the spatial suite shows the expert succeeding $5/8$ and $6/8$ on the two
table-height tasks but $0/8$ on the elevated-target task, where the goal sits on a stove and a moka
pot obstructs the approach. Failures there occur across every phase rather than at one, which is
the signature of a kinematic and controller-posture limit rather than of occlusion or collision
avoidance. The teacher's competence is therefore geometry-dependent, and this is a limitation of
the comparison rather than a property of privileged planners in general.

Restricting to the 18 scenes with training data does not rescue the result~--- the pooled figure is
already flat against base~--- but both slices should be reported, since the 24-scene number
conflates two distinct failures: scenes where the teacher had nothing to say, and scenes where it
spoke and was not understood.
